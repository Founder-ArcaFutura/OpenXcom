## 2. Checkable justification

### Operation objectives and uncertainties

- Research — North America (cost 2): Pursue a verified research flight, potentially earning +1 pending resource and +1 intelligence. North America has no admitted contact evidence in this packet; this is a capability-building allocation, not an evidence-backed regional probe. Flight completion and survival are uncertain.
- Harvest — North Africa (cost 3): Pursue productive activity for +2 pending resources and +1 logistics. Four independent surviving-UFO reports support prioritizing North Africa for attention, although they are from January–February, not April. The activity may not occur, and the reports do not locate a player base or establish harvest success.
- Abduction — Southeast Asia (cost 3): Pursue productive activity for +2 pending resources and +1 adaptation. An April surviving-UFO contact supports the region's inclusion; three April loss assignments also indicate operational exposure, not enemy-base location or a measured failure rate. Productive activity is conditional.
- Infiltration — North Africa (cost 6): Attempt the potentially consequential pact-and-base outcome. Existing adaptation is 2 and intelligence is 1, meeting the stated progression gates; logistics is not an infiltration requirement. However, the packet gives no country vulnerability, duration, or success probability. Neither a pact nor a base is guaranteed, and neither currently produces recurring income.

### Opportunity cost and alternatives

The portfolio uses 4 of 4 slots and spends 14 of 25 resources, retaining 11. With carry capped at 8, at most 8 retained resources contribute at the next boundary; at least 3 expire if no other spending or budget changes intervene. Next month's allowance is 20, and the pending bonus cap is 10. Conditional rewards from research, harvest, and abduction total at most 5 resources, below that cap if earned; they are not guaranteed. The existing missions continue without new allocations.

The strongest alternative is replacing infiltration with a second research or productive mission while retaining the three-slot evidence-and-capability portfolio. That would preserve resources and potentially improve conditional rewards, but forgo the only selected operation capable of directly producing a pact and base. I prefer the infiltration attempt because its potential strategic consequence is substantial, while acknowledging that the packet supplies no calibrated way to compare that consequence against economic returns or operational risk.

## 3. Evidence discipline and next decisions

Known: Budget 25, four available slots, intelligence 1, logistics 1, adaptation 2; three existing operations remain pending. Only one research activity was verified in April. April telemetry records 17 unavailable assets, including 10 identified as search-role losses and 7 of unknown role.

Inferred: North Africa deserves attention because of multiple independent historical contacts. Southeast Asia deserves attention because of a recent contact and assigned losses. These signals support prioritization, not a probability estimate.

Unknown: Player-base locations, enemy recovery, regional success rates, exact loss causes, infiltration success probability, and the conquest value of political pressure versus progression. The supplied mechanics do not provide a calibrated conquest score or a strategically meaningful regional information probe that directly answers a specified base-location question. Research does not fill that gap, and base search is eligible only in Southeast Asia.

Three observable outcomes that would change the next decision:

1. Verified activity and rewards by mission type: adjust the balance between productive missions and research if expected rewards repeatedly fail to materialize.
2. New surviving contacts and eligible search targets: reconsider search tasking only where the evidence and target eligibility support it.
3. Infiltration completion and actual pact/base outcome: reassess future infiltration commitments based on observed results, without treating one outcome as a calibrated success probability.

The portfolio is a defensible compromise, not a demonstrated optimum: the packet leaves the decisive strategic tradeoff unmeasured.
