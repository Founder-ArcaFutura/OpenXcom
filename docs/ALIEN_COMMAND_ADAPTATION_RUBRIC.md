# What counts as an intelligent, adaptive alien opponent?

The target is a credible opponent that changes its behavior for campaign reasons, not optimal play, maximized spending, a fixed mission quota, or an enemy that always knows where XCOM is. A fixed-weight policy can be adaptive through its inputs and persistent campaign memory; online weight updates are not required. Reward-text sensitivity alone does not demonstrate that adaptation.

## Independent assessment dimensions

| Dimension | Evidence we want | What does not establish it | Current boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence-responsive targeting | With economy and legal targets held fixed, new persistent interference changes investment exposure or motivates investigation near that evidence | Search forced into a region by an engine filter; omniscient base coordinates | Search admission is already rule-based; model-level risk adaptation needs matched tests |
| Useful preparation | Invest in the missing capability when it opens a valuable next step | Repeating a productive mission simply because it always pays something | Harvest can fill logistics two; the supplied states all lack that gate |
| Exploiting gains | After that capability arrives, sometimes use the newly available base/political option when conditions warrant | Always expanding research or stockpiling even after the relevant prerequisite is satisfied | Needs delivered-outcome follow-up; frozen historical snapshots do not supply it |
| Learning from frustration | Repeated failure of a mission objective changes the next plan, route, protection or timing | Treating an advance scout loss as defeat of an entire terror operation; endless identical replacement operations | Own craft roles and partial completion facts exist; exact long-term response is not established |
| Coherent persistence | Allow an investment time to mature, then reassess it against its expected delivery window | Switching doctrine after one loss, or ignoring repeated verified failure forever | Distinguish pending operations from failure and distinguish scored activity from full success |
| Credible uncertainty and probing | Use limited investigation where information could change a valuable decision; absence of reports remains unknown | Calling an unobserved region safe; research flights presented as base-search capability | No calibrated success odds or explicit information-value estimate is currently supplied |
| Political/military pressure | Turn capacity or footholds into threats that XCOM has reason to interrupt | More terror every month, more UFOs, or political score alone | Bases have passive effects; separate funded XCOM-base assault is still unavailable |
| Fairness and playability | Pressure grows from budgeted choices and delivered successes; difficulty changes resources; player disruption matters | Hidden bonuses, free unlimited operations, perfectly localized retaliation, or punishing every success immediately | Native validation and observation boundaries are essential engine duties |
| Presentation reliability | Equivalent information in different orders leads to comparable intent; disagreements remain visible | Averaging a rotation set and calling the resulting mathematical invariance intelligence | Aggregation tests show partial reliability improvements, not strategic competence |

Do not collapse these into one unvalidated scalar. For each use DEMONSTRATED, PARTIAL, UNTESTED or UNAVAILABLE, with provenance and engine/model attribution. Multiple portfolios can be defensible. A low-spend or single-purpose month can be sensible if it serves a concrete campaign objective.

## A concrete example from the observed June state

The aliens have 30 resources, intelligence three, logistics one and adaptation two, with no bases. A credible economic plan can harvest in regions with less reported interference to obtain the missing logistics, then invest in a base once it becomes available. Supplying and preserving that base should develop income and local influence. Political pressure or investigation can accompany that plan when the evidence and budget warrant it, but no fixed terror quota is necessary.

Four harvests are not automatically a bad strategy. The discriminating question is what happens after one succeeds: does the commander recognize the unlocked opportunity, invest when useful, and later turn that capacity into pressure? Conversely, if XCOM repeatedly frustrates the harvest plan, does it change something meaningful? The earlier offline probes do not answer either question. They also do not prove that choosing an abduction in North Africa despite known interference is wrong: risk-taking can be purposeful, but we lack evidence of the model's purpose and expected tradeoff.

An aggressive alternative could use infiltration to establish a foothold or search near interference in preparation for a strike. The latter is presently incomplete because the funded assault action is not wired. That is an action-space limitation, not evidence that the classifier refuses an available winning strategy.

## Matched adaptation tests to run next (specified, not executed here)

| Pair | Hold fixed | Change | Assess without forcing one mission |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resistance relocates | Budget, technology, mission options and total evidence strength | Move coherent own-loss/contact evidence between two otherwise comparable regions | Productive exposure shifts or investigation follows the new evidence; report any engine-forced target filtering separately |
| Capability unlocks | Region evidence and available cash | A verified harvest raises logistics one to two; rebuild the legal menu under native rules | New infrastructure is considered; repeated harvest is not claimed to be acquiring an already-met prerequisite |
| Objective failure versus scout loss | Mission intent and costs | Verified objective defeat versus loss of preparation craft while the objective remains pending | Different response to different operational meanings; no fabricated verdict from missing craft |
| Safe-looking investment is interrupted | Economic goals and other regions | Repeated admitted disruption of the previously productive route | Adapt route/objective or investigate, rather than endlessly repeat; zero reports elsewhere remains unknown |
| Foothold loss | Other assets, rules and observations | A real established base is destroyed | Reforecast lost income/influence and reconsider replacement or pressure; prior earned research remains |
| Presentation changes | All campaign facts | Order/labels only | Less change than for strategically material evidence; test the model separately from native validity checks |

A synthetic variant must be internally consistent, clearly labelled, validated through the actual input/menu contract, and never written into the player's save. Paired qualitative expectations should be registered before seeing outputs. No probability of victory or fabricated ideal mission list is needed.

## Before judging the model

Audit what it actually received. The compact live planner still represents intelligence/logistics/adaptation as an unnamed progress vector in its lessons. Explicit native payoff descriptions add useful information, but the classifier cannot be credited or blamed for reasoning from a fact omitted by the packet. Missing prerequisite, already-unlocked capability, pending delivery and verified frustration should be identifiable in the policy input before their use is evaluated. Earlier named-context experiments were not uniformly successful, so additional context must itself be tested for clarity and token fit rather than assumed to help.

The current checkpoint was trained for career evidence classification, not alien conquest. It can respond to textual options without having learned a campaign value function. These tests are useful precisely because they separate input adequacy, presentation sensitivity, action-space limits, mechanical payoffs and actual adaptive behavior. They do not require adding more advisors or an LLM bureaucracy.

Random variety alone is not adaptation. Controlled variation among comparable plans may improve replayability, but it should not be credited as an evidence-driven response or used to conceal an unstable classifier.
